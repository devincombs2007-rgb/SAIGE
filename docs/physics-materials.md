# Physical surface materials

A tire, a body or a falling object meets different things depending on what it touches. `include/saige/physics_material.hpp` gives the collision world a material per primitive without changing
`StaticCollision` (which stays geometry only).

* `Surface` (default, asphalt, wet asphalt, concrete, gravel, dirt, grass, sand, snow, ice) and `PhysicsMaterial` (tire grip, rolling resistance, body friction, restitution, density). The numbers are textbook ranges for
  dry conditions relative to dry asphalt (`VehicleConfig::tire_friction` remains the dry-asphalt value of the tire); they are approximations for a believable difference between surfaces, and a game replaces the table.
* `SurfaceMap`: one `Surface` per box and per triangle, indexed by the primitive ids the collision reports. A primitive the map does not cover reads as `Default`, so a stale map cannot crash a query.
* Combining: friction by the geometric mean, restitution by the larger; both symmetric. `Rubber` (grippy, lively) exists so that the restitution side of the rule has a material above the vehicle body's own.
* `Vehicle::step(input, dt, world, scratch, const SurfaceMap* surfaces = nullptr)`: the contact surface's `tire_grip` scales the tire's friction circle and its `rolling_resistance` the rolling term; and the body's collision with a primitive combines the body's `body_friction` and `body_restitution` with that primitive's material. With no map the
  result is bit-identical to before (`vehicle_surface` checks this against an all-Default and an all-Asphalt map, and the existing `vehicle` test is unchanged).

* `CapsuleInput::traction` (default 1, clamped to [0.02, 1]) scales the capsule controller's ground acceleration, and `foot_traction(material)` / `ground_traction(world, map, feet)` derive it from the surface under the feet (a ray straight down). The controller does not know about materials: the host asks the map and passes the number, so the default is bit-identical to before.

Threading and ownership: the map is read during the call only and is not retained; share it read-only between threads. Consumers: the vehicle (tires and body contacts) and the character (traction). Objects: `contact_velocity(velocity, normal | RayHit, object_material, surface_or_map, rest_speed)` is the response of a small falling object (debris, a dropped prop) meeting a surface: the normal part is reflected by the larger restitution of the pair (a lively ball stays lively on a dead floor), the tangential part loses the Coulomb impulse `mu (1 + e) |v_n|` with `mu` the geometric mean of the two frictions and never reverses, an impact at or below `rest_speed` (0.35 m/s) does not bounce so a settling object comes to rest, and no contact adds energy (`tests/contact_response_test.cpp`: bounce apex `e^2 h` through a real `StaticCollision` and `SurfaceMap`, 24,200 random contacts, mutation-checked: min instead of max restitution, no friction cap, no rest speed). It is not wired into Showcase 03, whose debris keeps its own constants so the delivered simulation baseline is unchanged; a later showcase passes its `SurfaceMap`. Remaining: wiring into a showcase, rolling resistance for rolling objects.

Evidence (`vehicle_surface` test, software build, Debug): from about 14 m/s the car stops in 11.6 m on asphalt, 12.9 m wet, 14.3 m gravel, 16.0 m grass, 17.5 m sand, 34.4 m snow and 83.5 m on ice (where friction
limits the stop it matches v^2 / (2 mu g) within 15 to 30 percent); braking from 15 m/s with grip on one side and ice on the other turns the car 7.2 degrees toward the grippy side (mirrored cases agree; even ground
turns it 1e-9 degrees); five seconds of full throttle reach 22.7 m/s on asphalt, 15.7 on grass and 2.5 on ice; coasting for five seconds loses 0.86, 2.63 and 3.97 m/s on asphalt, grass and sand. A head-on crash at 14 m/s into a wall bounces the car back at 8.2 m/s from a rubber wall against 2.1 m/s from concrete and 1.9 m/s with no map; an angled crash (a front corner first) spins it at 1.91 rad/s against rubber, 1.65 against concrete and 1.64 against ice (the spin comes mostly from the normal impulse at the corner, so friction changes it little). On ice a walking character has 0.85 m/s after half a second where concrete has its full 3.5 m/s, and slides 3.5 m to a stop against 0.27 m on concrete (`character_surface` test). Each effect was
shown to disappear when its term is removed from the vehicle (mutation).

## Vehicle handling options (`VehicleConfig`, `VehicleInput`)

Both are off by default, and neutral values are bit-identical to the vehicle before they existed (the Showcase 03 replay checks pass unchanged).

* **Tire load sensitivity** (`tire_load_sensitivity`, 0 to 1): a tire's grip coefficient is scaled by `1 - k (load / static load - 1)`, clamped to [0.5, 1.5], so load transferred onto the outside wheels in a corner does not buy back what the inside wheels lose (real tires: k about 0.1 to 0.3). In the reference turn at 22 m/s the lateral acceleration is 9.8 m/s^2 at k = 0, 8.6 at 0.3 and 7.7 at 0.6.
* **Handbrake** (`VehicleInput::handbrake`, 0 to 1; `handbrake_force`, `handbrake_grip`): extra brake force at the non-steered wheels together and a loss of their grip (their friction circle keeps `handbrake_grip` of its size at full handbrake). Stopping from 15 m/s: coasting more than 120 m, handbrake alone 50 m, full brake 13 m; pulled in a 16 m/s corner it brings the tail round (body sideslip 1.6 to 85 degrees, yaw rate 0.57 to 3.6 rad/s: at full handbrake held for 1.5 s the car spins); pulled on a straight line it stays straight.

Tested in `tests/vehicle_handling_test.cpp` (mutation-checked: grip loss, wheel selection, load sensitivity sign, handbrake force). Still not modelled: engine and gearbox curves, a differential, damage.
